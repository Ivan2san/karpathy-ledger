# Part 1: What current agents actually are

**P1. Agents are stochastic, fallible interns.** Powerful, but not employees. They handle macro-actions (implement a feature, refactor a subsystem, write tests) but still need orchestration, oversight, and human judgement on top. `[SEQ-B, DWA]`

**P2. December 2025 was the inflection.** Karpathy's personal ratio of writing-code to delegating-to-agents flipped from roughly 80/20 to 20/80 in a single month, and has not typed much code since. The chunks "just came out fine" and he could not remember the last time he corrected one. If your default workflow has not changed since late 2024, you are a full professional generation behind. `[SEQ-T 1:18, NP 1:50, SEQ-B]`

**P3. The autonomy slider, not the cliff.** Compilers, type checking, autocomplete, and agents are one continuum of automation, not a discontinuity. Humans are being raised up the abstraction ladder slowly. There is no single magic moment. `[DWA 00:36:53]`

**P4. Three working modes, pick by task.** Write-from-scratch (now outdated), autocomplete with the human as architect (Karpathy's default), and full agent delegation (specific settings only). Boilerplate, well-trodden patterns, and unfamiliar languages suit agents. Novel, intellectually intense, custom code suits autocomplete. `[DWA 00:29:45]`

**P57. AI psychosis is the felt state of unbounded leverage.** The phrase Karpathy uses for his current mode. Not a clinical claim. It is the feeling of being post-bottleneck: typing is no longer the constraint, capacity to direct is, and the ceiling is unknown so "everything is skill issue". The recognisable symptom is nervousness when you end the day with subscription tokens left over. `[NP 0:18, NP 2:00, NP 5:33]`
