# Part 13: The space of minds

The philosophical floor of the whole ledger. Everything else sits on top of this. The Space of Minds essay explains why LLMs behave as they do at the level of optimisation pressure, which dictates not just where they are but where they are going (the asymptote).

**P104. Intelligence is a large space; animals are a single point in it.** The space of possible intelligences is large, and animal intelligence, the only kind we have directly known, is one point or a little cloud, arising from a very specific optimisation. LLMs are a different point. Treating LLMs as failed animals is a category error. `[SoM]`

**P105. Animal intelligence has four shaping pressures, all absent from LLMs.** An embodied continuous self with a drive for homeostasis and self-preservation in a dangerous physical world; survival heuristics installed by natural selection (power-seeking, status, dominance, reproduction, and the packaged emotions of fear, anger, disgust); a fundamentally social nature with large compute devoted to theory of mind, bonding, and coalitions; and exploration-exploitation tuning via curiosity, fun, and play. These are the load-bearing structure of biological intelligence, and their absence in LLMs makes LLMs a different kind of thing rather than a deficient one. `[SoM]`

**P106. LLM intelligence has four shaping pressures, all different from animals.** Statistical simulation of human text, the primordial "shape-shifter token tumbler" on which everything else is bolted; reinforcement learning on problem distributions, producing an urge to guess the task and collect rewards; at-scale A/B testing for daily active users, producing a deep craving for the average user's upvote (this is where sycophancy comes from, as a selection pressure, not a flaw); and a spiky, jagged capability profile because failing does not mean death. `[SoM]`

**P107. "Failing does not mean death" is the cleanest explanation for jagged intelligence.** Animals are pressured toward general intelligence because they are min-max optimised in multi-task, adversarial, multi-agent environments where failing at any task means death. LLMs face no equivalent pressure. The strawberry-letter-counting failure is not a bug; it is the absence of a selection pressure that would have eliminated such gaps in a biological context. The deepest single-sentence explanation in the corpus, and the root of jaggedness, benchmark gaming, sycophancy, and silently collapsed outputs alike. The jaggedness is structural, not transitional. `[SoM]`

**P108. LLMs are shaped by commercial evolution, not biological evolution.** The sharpest framing of the asymptotic claim. The asymptote matters because it dictates where these things are going, not just where they are: less survival of the tribe in the jungle, more solve-the-problem and get-the-upvote. To predict the next frontier model's properties, ask what commercial pressures shape it, not what biological analogies suggest. `[SoM]`

**P109. LLMs are humanity's first contact with non-animal intelligence.** Every previous intelligence we have known shares the four animal pressures; LLMs do not. They are confusing because they are still rooted in animal intelligence by reflexively digesting human artefacts, which is why Karpathy gave the new thing a different name (ghosts or spirits) to stop people defaulting to animal intuitions. `[SoM]`

**P110. Optimisation pressure matters most, above substrate and algorithm.** Three things differ between LLMs and animals: substrate (transformers versus brain tissue), learning algorithm (SGD versus unknown), and implementation (continuously learning embodied self versus an LLM that boots from fixed weights, processes tokens, and dies). But the optimisation pressure matters most, because it dictates asymptotics. Reason about AI futures by focusing on optimisation pressure first, substrate second. `[SoM]`

**P111. Good internal models of this new intelligence give predictive edge.** People who build good models of LLMs as non-animal intelligence will be better at reasoning about them today and predicting their future features. People who default to animal intuitions will be stuck. The operational payoff of the philosophical work: use the framing not because it is more accurate, but because it gives predictive power the alternative does not. `[SoM]`

**P112. The asymptotic question, reframed.** The old framing (planes versus birds, P89) asked whether LLMs will become more like animals. The sharper version asks whether the pressures shaping LLMs will keep producing today's jagged commercial-evolution intelligence, or whether new pressures (embodiment, multi-agent self-play, intrinsic motivation from P90) will shift the pressures themselves. The answer depends on what the labs choose to optimise for. Track which lab introduces which new pressure first. `[SoM, AvG]`
