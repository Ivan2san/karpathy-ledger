## Summary
Karpathy points an LLM at Hacker News threads from exactly ten years earlier and grades the commenters on how well their predictions held up. The exercise works because the outcomes are now known, which makes the grading verifiable and cheap. The wider argument is about what becomes possible once retrospective synthesis costs nothing: if intelligence gets cheap enough, a complete reconstruction of the past becomes feasible, and the obscurity people have quietly relied on stops protecting them.

## Key claims
- A class of information processing with no classical equivalent: read a decade-old discussion, resolve it against what actually happened, and score the participants (P24).
- The task is verifiable by construction, because the outcomes are already known, which is what makes the grading trustworthy and cheap (P22).
- If intelligence becomes cheap enough, perfect reconstruction and synthesis of the past becomes possible (P116).
- Obscurity has been doing quiet protective work, and that protection weakens as the cost of looking falls. Karpathy's practical advice is to behave well because the models are reading, or the people using them are (P116).
