## Summary
A response to the Sutton interview on Dwarkesh. Karpathy argues that LLMs are not animals being grown but ghosts being summoned: statistical distillations of human text shaped by a fundamentally different optimisation process than evolution. He grants Sutton's point that LLMs are not cleanly "bitter lesson pilled" while arguing pretraining is the practical answer to the cold-start problem.

## Key claims
- LLMs are a different point in the space of intelligences, not failed animals (P14, P104).
- Pretraining is "crappy evolution", a candidate solution to needing dense supervision over billions of parameters (P86).
- Animal learning is mostly maturation and finetuning on evolved priors; nothing starts tabula rasa (P87).
- The bitter lesson is platonic, not achievable in practice today (P88).
- Ghosts may converge toward animals or diverge permanently, like planes versus birds (P89).
- Ideas worth borrowing: intrinsic motivation, fun, curiosity, empowerment, self-play, culture (P90).
- In-context learning is a form of test-time adaptation; memory files are its substrate (P91).
