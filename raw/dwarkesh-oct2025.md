# Dwarkesh Patel interview

- Tag: DWA
- Type: podcast
- Author: Andrej Karpathy (with Dwarkesh Patel)
- Date: Oct 2025
- Weight: direct
- Link: https://www.dwarkesh.com/

## Summary
A long-form conversation. Karpathy argues for a "decade of agents" rather than a year, explains why reinforcement learning is weak, why agents struggle with code never written before, and why deployment follows a march of nines. Also the origin of several mental models: weights as hazy recollection versus context as working memory, and the cognitive core.

## Key claims
- Agents are fallible interns; the autonomy slider, not a cliff (P1, P3, P4).
- Agents are bad at code never written before (P5).
- RL "sucks supervision through a straw"; LLM judges get gamed; outputs are silently collapsed (P8, P9, P10).
- Weights are hazy recollection; context is working memory (P13).
- The cognitive core may be about a billion parameters (P15).
- The march of nines; demos are not products; hidden human-in-the-loop is normal (P34, P35, P37).
- Build it or you do not understand it; re-explain to deepen (P45, P46).
